# Ad Atomica website content review

Working notes for iterating on the website's positioning, proof, and conversion copy. These are content decisions, not approved replacement copy.

## 1. Hero proposition

### Current issue

“Drug discovery reinvented” is memorable, but it does not tell a first-time visitor what Ad Atomica does, who it is for, or what outcome it provides.

### Content to define

- One plain-language description of the platform.
- The primary audience: biotech/pharma teams, academic groups, or both.
- The most defensible customer outcome.
- A primary action such as “Discuss a target” or “Assess programme fit.”
- A secondary action that explains the platform before asking for contact.

### Working direction

Keep “Drug discovery reinvented” as the brand line, supported by one sentence that identifies the service, audience, and outcome. Avoid adding unsupported speed or cost claims to the hero.

## 2. Evidence and scientific proof

### Current issue

Claims about delivering candidates in weeks, reducing costs, improving predictive accuracy, and finding otherwise missed scaffolds are not accompanied by evidence.

### Evidence to gather

- Number of molecules designed, synthesised, or tested.
- Representative design-to-test cycle time.
- Experimentally validated hits or series.
- Measured improvement between optimisation rounds.
- A representative programme or anonymised case study.
- Publications, patents, grants, collaborations, or conference presentations.
- Partner or institutional names that may be disclosed.

### Decision needed

For every quantitative or comparative claim, choose one of:

1. Support it with attributable evidence.
2. Qualify it precisely.
3. Remove or soften it.

## 3. Commercial proposition

### Current issue

The site presents Ad Atomica as a platform company, discovery-services provider, academic collaborator, internal pipeline company, and investor opportunity. The primary business model is unclear.

### Content to define

- The primary commercial offer.
- Where an engagement begins and ends.
- What the client supplies.
- What Ad Atomica owns and delivers.
- Who performs synthesis and assays.
- Typical programme stages and decision points.
- How collaboration, IP, and grant-funded academic work differ.
- Whether the internal pipeline is central to the proposition or supporting context.

## 4. “How We Work” narrative

### Current issue

The section currently describes two customer categories rather than the engagement process.

### Questions the section should answer

1. What information does a partner bring?
2. How is programme fit assessed?
3. What is designed in the first cycle?
4. How are compounds synthesised and tested?
5. How are results incorporated into the next cycle?
6. What constitutes a successful handoff or programme outcome?

The academic partnership and discovery-services pathways can remain, but should sit beneath a clear shared process.

## 5. Audience pathways

### Current issue

Academic groups, biotech/pharma teams, investors, and potential partners receive the same generic invitation to contact the company.

### Content to define

- The priority audience for the homepage.
- A specific next step for target owners.
- A distinct route for academic collaborations.
- Whether investor enquiries require a visible pathway.
- What information Ad Atomica wants someone to include in an initial enquiry.

## 6. Calls to action

### Current issue

“Start a conversation” is friendly but does not communicate the outcome of clicking. The page also gives few opportunities to act before the final section.

### Options to evaluate

- Discuss a target
- Assess programme fit
- Explore a collaboration
- Speak with the team
- View the platform process

The final contact section should set expectations for what happens after contact and what information is useful to provide.

## 7. Team and advisors

### Current issue

The credentials are strong, but the biographies are lengthy relative to the platform and commercial proposition.

### Content direction

- Keep short homepage biographies focused on each person's relevance to delivery.
- Retain full biographies for a dedicated team page or optional expanded view.
- Connect advisor expertise explicitly to platform validation, chemistry, clinical strategy, or programme decisions.
- Confirm that citation counts, trial stages, and prior-company claims are current and supportable.

## 8. Company credibility and footer information

### Content to gather

- Legal company name and registration details.
- Registered office or required jurisdictional information.
- Privacy policy.
- LinkedIn and other active company channels.
- Publications, news, careers, or partner links where applicable.
- Consent/privacy implications of embedded third-party media.

If Ad Atomica is a UK limited company, confirm the statutory information that must appear on the website.

## 9. Recommended narrative order

Once the content above is available, evaluate this homepage sequence:

1. Brand line, plain-language proposition, and CTA.
2. Defensible evidence or proof points.
3. What the platform delivers.
4. How the closed loop works.
5. Engagement pathways.
6. Representative programme or case study.
7. Condensed team and advisors.
8. Specific final CTA.

## 10. Content approval checklist

Before publishing revised copy, confirm:

- The primary customer and commercial offer are explicit.
- Every material scientific or comparative claim is supportable.
- Terminology matches how customers describe their programmes.
- The distinction between hits, leads, candidates, and preclinical readiness is accurate.
- Internal pipeline language does not conflict with service or partnership positioning.
- The CTA describes a real next step.
- Legal, privacy, and company information is complete.
